The Heart of the Harbor: My Ministry to Global Seafarers

By Fr. JP Heath July 23, 2025 4 min read Malmö, Sweden

It is a truly unique calling, one that frequently takes me into the industrial heart of the maritime world, connecting with extraordinary individuals from all corners of the globe. As a chaplain in the Port of Malmö, my days are not spent inside a quiet parish sanctuary, but climbing the steep steel gangways of cargo carriers, container vessels, and bulk carriers moored along the Öresund.

Recently, I had the pleasure of being joined aboard by Carl Adam Landström, the organist at St. Petri Church, where I also serve as priest. Bringing music, accompaniment, and quiet solidarity into the crew mess halls of ships that have been at sea for weeks creates moments of profound, unexpected grace.

Ninety percent of world trade moves across the oceans, transported by international crews who endure nine to twelve months far away from their spouses, children, and homelands. They live in constant motion, contending with rough seas, engine noise, and deep physical isolation. When a ship docks for only eighteen hours to discharge grain or steel, the crew rarely has time to clear customs or visit the city.

“Pastoral presence aboard a vessel does not begin with dogma. It begins with asking: ‘How is your family? Have you spoken to your children? What do you need today?’”

Our visits provide practical essentials—local SIM cards to phone home, fresh reading material, chocolate, and transportation to the seafarers’ center. But more than goods, we offer a ministry of listening. In the tight quarters of the ship's galley, seafarers share the grief of missed birthdays, the anxiety of political unrest in their home nations, and the quiet dignity of their labor.

In these encounters, the ancient Christian tradition of hospitality is renewed. The gangway becomes an altar, the ship’s table becomes a place of communion, and the forgotten workers of the global economy are reminded that they are seen, honored, and held in love.
