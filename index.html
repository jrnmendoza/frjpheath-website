Biographical Journey

A Life Formed Across Continents

An editorial journey through the defining chapters of JP’s life and ministry—from early formation in Southern Africa to international advocacy and maritime chaplaincy in Scandinavia.

Chapter 01 · Formation

Roots: Namibia & Southern Africa

Born into an Afrikaans family in Namibia, formation unfolded across the expansive dry horizons of Southern Africa and Potchefstroom. In an era structured by rigid racial classification and state violence, early conscience wrestled with scripture, prophetic justice, and the costs of discipleship.

Academic and biblical studies at Potchefstroom University grounded JP in theological rigor, while the unfolding struggle for justice in Southern Africa permanently shaped his understanding of God's option for the marginalized.

Chapter 02 · Ordination

Calling: The Altar & St. George’s Cathedral

Ordained Deacon in 1994 and Priest in 1995 within the Anglican Church of Southern Africa, JP entered ordained ministry at St. George’s Cathedral in Cape Town—the revered "People's Cathedral" led by Archbishop Desmond Tutu during South Africa’s historic transition to democracy.

Serving at the heart of the mother city meant preaching the Gospel at the crossroads of national reconciliation, racial integration, and liturgical renewal.

Chapter 03 · Prophetic Vulnerability

The Margins: Pulpit Disclosure & The Theology of Dignity

In 2000, amidst pervasive stigma and societal fear during the HIV pandemic in Southern Africa, JP received his own diagnosis. Refusing secrecy or institutional shame, he disclosed his status from the cathedral pulpit—becoming one of the first ordained Anglican priests on the continent to speak openly.

“Grace is not an institutional prize for the respectable; it is the radical, loving presence of God with the wounded and marginalized.”

This moment fundamentally recast his vocation into a lifelong theological defense of human dignity, bodily integrity, and unconditional inclusion.

Chapter 04 · Global Accompaniment

A Wider World: INERELA+, Dialogue & Human Rights

Vulnerability grew into global accompaniment. JP co-founded and helped direct INERELA+ (International Network of Religious Leaders Living with or Personally Affected by HIV and AIDS), walking alongside religious leaders across multiple continents.

His work expanded into international ecumenical dialogue with the World Council of Churches, human rights advocacy, mediation, LGBTQ+ inclusion, and participatory training that challenged religious condemnation through scripture and shared humanity.

Chapter 05 · Maritime Chaplaincy

Malmö: Ships, Crews & The Everyday Waterfront

In 2019, ministry relocated to the Öresund coast of Sweden. As priest in the Church of Sweden (St. Petri parish) and maritime chaplain with Sjömanskyrkan in the industrial port of Malmö, JP climbs steep steel gangways to meet multinational crews aboard bulk carriers and container vessels.

“Ninety percent of world trade is carried by sea, moved by crews who spend nine to twelve months far from home. Pastoral care here means climbing the gangway with Wi-Fi cards, warm listening, and standing with those who sustain our society in silence.”

Chapter 06 · John 10:10

Life in Abundance: The Sacred Hospitality of the Table

For JP, cooking and hospitality are not detached pastimes—they are the material practice of pastoral theology. Around the stove and table, strangers become companions—the Latin com-panis, those who break bread together.

From traditional Easter hot cross buns rooted in family memory to slow-cooked winter stews in Malmö, the shared table serves as an everyday altar of reconciliation, friendship, and joy.

Authored Volume · Abrahamic Faiths & Sexuality

Behold, I Make All Things New

What do the sacred texts of Judaism, Christianity and Islam really say in regard to human sexuality?

An insightful theological and scriptural window into how modern Judaic, Christian, and Muslim scholars unpack the biblical and scriptural verses most often cited to condemn homosexuality. Born out of a historic panel discussion on human sexuality at the Church of Sweden’s 2015 Festival of Theology, the volume was authored for theologians, academics, LGBTQ+ persons, their families, and faith communities seeking an understanding of God’s unconditional grace.

“Faith cannot be separated from our sexuality and there are no aliens in the household of God. Working on an interfaith basis allows us to examine more deeply the things that bind us together in our One God. I strongly recommend this book.” — Archbishop Njongonkulu Ndungane, Former Archbishop of Cape Town

Authored Volume · Karmic Faiths & Global Queer Emancipation

I Am Divine. So Are You

How Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism and Hinduism affirm the dignity of queer identities and sexualities

Following the 2015 Festival of Theology in Sweden, this landmark volume expands the dialogue between world religions and human sexuality to a truly global level by incorporating the Karmic faiths—Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Hinduism—which represent nearly a third of the world’s population. The theology of Karmic traditions is examined at the intersection of scripture, culture, rituals, and lived realities, revealing ancient recognitions and acceptances of fluidity in human sexuality.

“A landmark publication that recasts world religions—especially Karmic faiths—as allies rather than adversaries of queer emancipation, directly informing secular and international legal movements for LGBTQ+ human rights around the world.” — Global Festival of Theology Consultation
